# Research Log: Week 4 - Pilot Audit, Hypothesis Evaluation, and Protocol Freeze

## What I Learned
During Week 4, I executed a comprehensive five-point audit of the Stage 3 pilot study (`notebooks/04_pilot_analysis.ipynb`), verifying that all 81 experimental runs (9 clean baselines and 72 poisoned conditions across seeds 0, 1, and 2) completed with exact poison counts, zero missing values, strict clean-test separation, and zero preprocessing leakage (`StandardScaler` fit strictly on `X_train`). Reading Schwarzschild et al. (2021), *"Just How Toxic Is Data Poisoning?"*, reinforced why consistent evaluation conditions—shared splits, identical poisoned samples across models, and fixed hyperparameters—are essential for fair benchmarking. Evaluating our three preliminary hypotheses confirmed that targeted spam-to-ham poisoning reduces spam recall far more aggressively than random flipping (collapsing Logistic Regression recall from 89.72% to 19.22% at 20% poisoning while keeping precision above 93%), and demonstrated via $k$-NN ($k=10$) neighborhood disagreement distributions that flipped spam samples remain distinguishable in feature space.

## What Failed & Contradictory Findings
Rather than hiding unexpected trends, I analyzed a key contradictory finding at 20% targeted poisoning: although Random Forest was the strongest model at 0%–10% poisoning, its spam recall dropped to 30.54% at 20% targeted poisoning, falling below the single Decision Tree (51.10%). Because 20% targeted poisoning flips 50.8% of all training spam (736 of 1,450 spam emails) to ham, ensemble subspace averaging shifts global majority votes below 0.5, whereas a single depth-8 tree retains isolated pure leaves on extreme indicator features (`char_freq_$`).
