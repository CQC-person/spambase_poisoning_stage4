# Research Log: Week 5 (Stage 4 Week 1) - Full 10-Seed Attack Experiment and Analysis

## What I Learned
During Week 1 of Stage 4, I executed the complete 10-seed poisoning benchmark (`seeds = 0..9`) under our frozen experimental protocol, generating all 270 required result rows (`results/attack_results.csv`: 30 clean-baseline rows and 240 poisoned-condition rows) with zero missing values. Aggregating performance via cross-seed mean and sample standard deviation (`notebooks/05_full_attack_analysis.ipynb`) confirmed our core Stage 3 hypotheses with tighter statistical precision. Under 20% random label flipping (availability attack), spam recall declines moderately across all three models: Random Forest maintains $87.69\% \pm 2.08\%$ (down from $91.98\% \pm 1.44\%$), Decision Tree retains $80.91\% \pm 2.31\%$, and Logistic Regression retains $79.89\% \pm 2.68\%$. Conversely, 20% targeted spam-to-ham flipping (integrity evasion attack) causes a severe collapse in spam recall—dropping Logistic Regression to $21.54\% \pm 3.64\%$, Random Forest to $31.46\% \pm 2.50\%$, and Decision Tree to $45.07\% \pm 9.67\%$—while spam precision remains deceptively high ($94.35\%$–$98.81\%$).

## Unexpected Findings & Epistemic Separation
In our 10-seed evaluation, the crossover between Decision Tree and Random Forest under targeted poisoning persisted: at 10% targeted poisoning, Decision Tree achieved slightly higher mean spam recall ($77.60\% \pm 3.47\%$ vs. $76.06\% \pm 1.56\%$, higher in 6/10 seeds) though lower F1 ($84.26\%$ vs. $85.47\%$); at 20% targeted poisoning, Decision Tree outperformed Random Forest in 9/10 seeds on both recall ($45.07\%$ vs. $31.46\%$) and F1 ($60.46\%$ vs. $47.67\%$), failing only on Seed 7 ($23.97\%$ vs. $30.30\%$). In drafting the `Attack Results` section of `paper/main.tex`, I strictly separated this direct empirical observation from its possible explanation (ensemble vote dilution vs. isolated greedy splits), noting that causal confirmation requires vote-distribution or leaf-level evidence.
